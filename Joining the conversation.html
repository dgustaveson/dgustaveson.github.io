How to use this survey

  1. Read each statement as it appears on screen.
  2. Choose one:
    • Agree — you support the statement
    • Disagree — you don't support it
    • Pass — skip it if it's unclear, confusing, or you're not sure
  3. Want to add your own short statement? Type it in the box and submit. Other participants can then vote on it the same way.
  4. After at least 7 people have voted:
    • You'll see a display of clustered opinion results.
    • The blue dot is you. Once the chart appears, you'll see shapes representing groups of people who tend to vote the same way — these are called opinion clusters.
    • Your position as the blue dot shows you which group you're closest to, or whether you land somewhere in between.
  5. When the groups are displayed, you can click on buttons under them to see how groups identify. These interactive buttons under the opinion map allow you to explore the consensus and all the specific group perspectives within the conversation:
    • Majority Opinion: Clicking this highlights consensus statements—opinions where a large majority across all opinion groups agree (either overwhelmingly agreeing or disagreeing), regardless of their general differences.
    • Group (A, B, [...]): Clicking one of these lettered buttons focuses on a specific cluster of voters. It highlights statements that define that particular group, showing which opinions set them apart or bring them together.
    • In the example image, it shows "Statement (0, 3, 6, 16, 33...)": These numbered buttons correspond to specific submitted statements. Clicking a statement number reveals how different groups voted on that exact statement, showing the percentage of agreement or disagreement across the map.

Votes are anonymous.

You can't reply directly to someone else's statement — You can only vote on these statements or add your own for others to vote on.

There's no wrong answer. Passing is fine.
